Uned 1 Helô, sut dych chi? ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Good afternoon. How are you? (Formal/Plural)

Prynhawn da. Sut dych chi?

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Wedi blino'n lân.

Exhausted / Completely tired.

'n lân' comes from 'glân' (clean) and adds emphasis — 'wedi blino'n lân' = utterly worn out.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I am very well, thanks. How are you? (Informal)

Da iawn, diolch. Sut wyt ti?

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Pwy dych chi?

Who are you? (Formal/Plural)

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I live in Swansea. Where do you live? (Formal)

Dw i'n byw yn Abertawe. Ble dych chi'n byw?

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Bore da, Siân dw i. Dw i'n byw yng Nghaerdydd.

Good morning, I am Siân. I live in Cardiff.

Note the nasal mutation 'yng Nghaerdydd'.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Terrible!

Ofnadwy!

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Noswaith dda, Croeso i Gymru.

Good evening, Welcome to Wales.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Ti vs Chi: Welsh has two words for 'you'. Use 'ti' for one person you know well (family, friends, pets, children). Use 'chi' for more than one person, or for one adult you don't know well (formal).
  • Verb First (mostly): Welsh generally puts the verb first. In "Dw i'n byw", 'Dw i' (I am) is the verb acting as the anchor.
  • Name Order: When introducing yourself, the name comes first: "[Name] dw i." (Literally: [Name] am I).
  • South Wales specific: In South Wales, it is standard to say 'dych chi', whereas you will hear 'dach chi' in North Wales. Stick with 'dych' for this course.
Uned 2 Wyt ti'n hoffi coffi? ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I like playing rugby, but I don't like playing football.

Dw i'n hoffi chwarae rygbi, ond dw i ddim yn hoffi chwarae pêl-droed.

'lico' can also be used instead of 'hoffi' in South Wales.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Wyt ti'n lico siocled?

Do you like chocolate? (Informal)

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Do you watch Emmerdale? (Formal) -> No.

Dych chi'n gwylio Emmerdale? Nac ydw.

Use 'Nac ydw' to answer 'No, I don't' to a Dych chi/Wyt ti question.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Dych chi'n dysgu Cymraeg? Ydw.

Are you learning Welsh? Yes.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I enjoy going to the cinema.

Dw i'n mwynhau mynd i'r sinema.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Wyt ti'n darllen llyfr? Nac ydw.

Are you reading a book? No.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I don't like drinking beer.

Dw i ddim yn hoffi yfed cwrw.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Dw i ddim yn gweithio heddiw.

I am not working today.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • The linking 'yn': To connect 'Dw i' (I am) to a verbnoun like 'darllen' (reading), you MUST use 'yn' (shortened to 'n after a vowel). Dw i'n darllen.
  • The Negative: To say 'I am not', insert 'ddim' between 'Dw i' and the linking 'yn'. -> Dw i ddim yn...
  • Answering Yes/No: Welsh doesn't have a single word for Yes or No. You answer using the verb that was asked. If asked "Wyt ti / Dych chi...?" (Are you?), you answer "Ydw" (I am) or "Nac ydw" (I am not).
Uned 3 Dych chi eisiau paned? ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I want a cuppa, please.

Dw i eisiau paned, os gwelwch chi'n dda.

In South Wales, 'Dw i'n moyn paned' is also very common in speech (the course book itself uses eisiau).
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Ble dych chi'n gweithio?

Where do you work?

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Siôn wants to go home.

Mae Siôn eisiau mynd adre. / Mae Siôn yn moyn mynd adre.

Start the sentence with 'Mae' when talking about a third person.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Dych chi eisiau bwyd? Nac ydw, diolch.

Do you want food? No, thanks.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I don't want to watch television.

Dw i ddim eisiau gwylio'r teledu.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Mae Ceri eisiau hufen iâ.

Ceri wants ice cream.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Does he want coffee?

Ydy e eisiau coffi?

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Wyt ti'n moyn dysgu?

Do you want to learn? (Informal)

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • The Golden Rule of Eisiau: Never, ever put 'yn' before 'eisiau'. It is ALWAYS "Dw i eisiau", never "Dw i'n eisiau".
  • Eisiau vs Moyn: The course book uses 'eisiau' throughout, but you will hear 'moyn' all over South Wales. Because 'moyn' is a standard verbnoun, it does take 'yn': Dw i'n moyn paned. Both are completely acceptable!
  • Third Person Rule: When stating that a specific person wants something, start the sentence with Mae. Mae Aled eisiau coffi.
Uned 4 Mynd a dod (Going & Coming) ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I come from Pontypridd.

Dw i'n dod o Bontypridd.

P mutates to B after 'o'.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Dw i'n mynd i Gaerdydd.

I am going to Cardiff.

C mutates to G after 'i'.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

We are going to town tomorrow.

'Dyn ni'n mynd i'r dre yfory.

Dre (from Tref) mutates even after i'r because it's a feminine noun.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

O ble dych chi'n dod yn wreiddiol?

Where do you come from originally?

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I am going to the pub later.

Dw i'n mynd i'r dafarn wedyn.

Tafarn is feminine, so T -> D after 'i'r'.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Ble dych chi'n mynd heno?

Where are you going tonight?

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Are you going to Carmarthen today? (Formal)

Dych chi'n mynd i Gaerfyrddin heddiw?

C -> G after 'i'.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Dw i'n dod o Ddinbych.

I come from Denbigh.

D -> Dd after 'o'.
Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Prepositions and Mutations: The words 'i' (to) and 'o' (from) cause the Treiglad Meddal (Soft Mutation) to the word directly following them.
  • Soft Mutation Rules: C→G, P→B, T→D, G→(drops), B→F, D→Dd, M→F, Ll→L, Rh→R.
    (e.g., o + Pontypridd = o Bontypridd).
  • 'i'r' (to the) Exception: Normally, adding 'the' (yr/r) blocks the mutation. E.g., 'i'r capel' (to the chapel - C does not mutate). HOWEVER, if the noun is feminine (like tref/town, tafarn/pub), it STILL mutates! Hence: i'r dre.
Uned 5 Beth wnest ti ddoe? (Past Tense - Gwneud) ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

What did you do yesterday? (Informal)

Beth wnest ti ddoe?

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Gwnes i wylio'r teledu neithiwr.

I watched television last night.

Literally: I did watch the television. Note the soft mutation 'gwylio' -> 'wylio'.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Did you make dinner last night? (Formal) -> Yes.

Wnaethoch chi ginio neithiwr? Do.

Universal past-tense Yes is 'Do'.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Wnest ti chwarae golff dydd Sadwrn? Naddo.

Did you play golf on Saturday? No.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I did a lot of work today.

Gwnes i lawer o waith heddiw.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Gwnaethon ni lanhau'r tŷ.

We cleaned the house.

Statements keep the G (Gwnaethon ni); the question drops it (Wnaethon ni?). glanhau -> lanhau after 'ni'.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I made a cup of tea.

Gwnes i baned o de.

paned -> baned.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Wnaethon nhw gysgu? Do.

Did they sleep? Yes.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • The Auxiliary Past Tense: An easy way to speak in the past tense is to use the verb 'Gwneud' (to do/make) + the action verb. "I did read" = Gwnes i ddarllen.
  • Mutation after the Subject: In this pattern, the main action verb takes a Soft Mutation because it follows the subject pronoun. Gwnes i ddarllen (darllen), Gwnes i wylio (gwylio).
  • Yes and No in the Past: Forget Ydw/Nac Ydw for a moment. In the past tense, you simply say Do (Yes, I/you/we did) and Naddo (No, I/you/we didn't) for EVERYTHING.
Uned 6 Sut mae'r tywydd? ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

What's the weather like today?

Sut mae'r tywydd heddiw?

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Mae hi'n bwrw glaw ac mae hi'n wyntog.

It is raining and it is windy.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

It is sunny, but it is cold.

Mae hi'n heulog, ond mae hi'n oer.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Maen nhw'n gorfod gweithio dydd Llun.

They have to work on Monday.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

He is playing football on Saturday.

Mae e'n chwarae pêl-droed dydd Sadwrn.

In South Wales, use 'Mae e'n' for 'He is'.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Mae'r tywydd yn ofnadwy dros y penwythnos.

The weather is terrible over the weekend.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

It's cloudy. I have to stay in.

Mae hi'n gymylog. Dw i'n gorfod aros i mewn.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Dydd Iau, dydd Gwener, dydd Sadwrn, dydd Sul.

Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • The Weather Pronoun: For weather (and later, time) Welsh uses the feminine pronoun 'hi' for 'it' — not because 'tywydd' is feminine (it isn't). Mae hi'n oer. Many people just say Mae'n oer.
  • Third Person Pronouns: We expand beyond 'I' and 'You' here. Mae e'n (He is), Mae hi'n (She is), Maen nhw'n (They are). Note: In North Wales, they say 'Mae o'n' for 'He is'.
  • Gorfod (Having to): A very useful verb to express obligation. "Dw i'n gorfod" (I must / I have to). Just link it like a normal verb!
Uned 7 Ydy hi'n gweithio? (3rd Person Questions) ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Is he watching rugby? -> Yes.

Ydy e'n gwylio rygbi? Ydy.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Ydy hi'n gweithio heddiw? Nac ydy.

Is she working today? No.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Is Aled tired? -> Yes.

Ydy Aled wedi blino? Ydy.

'Wedi blino' acts like an adjective here.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Dyw'r bwyd ddim yn dda.

The food is not good.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

He is not going out tonight.

Dyw e ddim yn mynd mas heno.

Use 'Dyw e ddim' for 'He is not'. 'Mas' is South Walian for 'out' (allan).
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Ydyn nhw'n yfed coffi? Nac ydyn.

Are they drinking coffee? No.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Are they from Cardiff? -> Yes.

Ydyn nhw'n dod o Gaerdydd? Ydyn.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Dyn nhw ddim yn hoffi caws.

They don't like cheese.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Changing Mae to Ydy: To turn a 3rd person statement (Mae e...) into a question, swap 'Mae' for 'Ydy'. Ydy e'n gweithio?
  • Answering 3rd Person Questions: As always, answer with the verb asked. "Ydy e...?" -> Ydy (Yes, he is) / Nac ydy (No, he is not). For 'they' (Ydyn nhw?), the answers are Ydyn / Nac ydyn.
  • The 3rd Person Negative: To say He/She/It is NOT, use Dyw instead of Mae. Dyw e ddim (He is not). Dyw hi ddim (She is not). Dyw'r tywydd ddim (The weather is not).
Uned 8 Siarad ar y ffôn ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

May I speak to Sioned, please?

Gaf i siarad â Sioned, os gwelwch chi'n dda?

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Gaf i baned? Cewch.

May I have a cuppa? Yes you may.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Who is calling?

Pwy sy'n galw?

'sy' is used instead of 'mae' when the question word (Pwy) is the subject.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Mae e'n gweithio mewn siop.

He works in a shop.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

She works in the college.

Mae hi'n gweithio yn y coleg.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Gaf i adael neges?

May I leave a message?

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Hold the line.

Daliwch y lein.

Or simply 'Un funud' (one moment). The course uses 'lein' for a phone line.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Na chewch, mae'n ddrwg 'da fi.

No you may not, I am sorry.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Asking Permission (Gaf i): Gaf i... means 'May I have...' or 'May I...'. Answer someone you call chi with Cewch / Na chewch, and someone you call ti with Cei / Na chei.
  • Mewn vs Yn y: In Welsh, 'in a' (undefined) and 'in the' (defined) use different words. Mewn ysgol = In a school. Yn yr ysgol = In the school. (Yn y becomes yn yr before a vowel).
  • Pwy sy'n: When asking "Who is [doing something]?", we don't say "Pwy mae". We must use sy (a special form of the verb 'to be'). Pwy sy'n galw? (Who is calling?)
Uned 9 Prynon ni fara (Short Form Past Tense) ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I watched a film last night.

Gwyliais i ffilm neithiwr.

Short form past tense: Stem (gwyli) + ais + i.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Prynodd e lyfr ddoe.

He bought a book yesterday.

Notice 'llyfr' mutates to 'lyfr' because it is the direct object following a short form past tense verb.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

We bought bread and milk.

Prynon ni fara a llaeth.

Bara -> Fara (soft mutation as direct object).
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Welon nhw'r gêm? Do.

Did they see the game? Yes.

Question, so gwelon -> welon.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Did you read the paper? (Formal) -> No.

Ddarllenoch chi'r papur? Naddo.

Questions in the short form past tense take a soft mutation on the verb! D->Dd.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Dechreuon ni am naw o'r gloch.

We started at nine o'clock.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

She drank tea this morning.

Yfodd hi de y bore 'ma.

Te -> De (direct object mutation).
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Gweithion nhw yn y dre.

They worked in the town.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Short Form Endings: Instead of using 'Gwnes i' + verb, you add an ending directly to the verb stem. I (-ais i), You (-aist ti), He/She (-odd e/hi), We (-on ni), You plural (-och chi), They (-on nhw).
  • Direct Object Mutation: If a noun immediately follows a short-form past tense verb, it takes a Soft Mutation! Yfodd e goffi (He drank coffee [coffi]).
  • Question Mutations: To turn a short form statement into a question, mutate the first letter of the verb. Prynon ni (We bought) -> Brynon ni? (Did we buy?).
Uned 10 Es i i'r dre (Irregular Past - Mynd) ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I went to the cinema yesterday.

Es i i'r sinema ddoe.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Aeth e i'r gwaith y bore 'ma.

He went to work this morning.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

We went to a restaurant on Saturday night.

Aethon ni i fwyty nos Sadwrn.

Bwyty -> Fwyty (Mutation after 'i').
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Est ti i'r dre ddoe? Do.

Did you go to town yesterday? Yes.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I didn't go out at all.

Es i ddim mas o gwbl.

'mas' (South) vs 'allan' (North). 'o gwbl' means 'at all'.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Aethon nhw i Loegr ar y trên.

They went to England on the train.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Did you go to the hospital? (Formal) -> No.

Aethoch chi i'r ysbyty? Naddo.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Aeth hi ddim i'r dosbarth nos Fercher.

She didn't go to the class on Wednesday night.

Using 'ddim' immediately after the subject makes it negative.
Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Irregular Verb 'Mynd' (To go): The past tense of this verb does not follow the normal rules. You must memorize these forms: Es i (I went), Est ti (You went), Aeth e/hi (He/She went), Aethon ni (We went), Aethoch chi (You went), Aethon nhw (They went).
  • Negative of Irregular verbs: To say you *didn't* go, just drop 'ddim' in after the pronoun. Es i ddim (I didn't go). Aeth e ddim (He didn't go).
  • No Mutation for Questions: Unlike regular verbs, you do not mutate 'Aeth...' to ask a question. Aeth e i'r gêm? (Did he go to the game?). The tone of your voice makes it a question!
Uned 11 Ces i dost i frecwast (Past - Cael) ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I had porridge for breakfast.

Ces i uwd i frecwast.

Ges i uwd is just as common. brecwast → frecwast after 'i'.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Beth gest ti i ginio?

What did you have for lunch? (Informal)

cinio → ginio after 'i'.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Did you have soup? (Formal) -> Yes.

Gawsoch chi gawl? Do.

Or Gaethoch chi gawl? The object mutates: cawl → gawl.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Cafodd Pat goffi yn y caffi.

Pat had coffee in the café.

Or Gaeth Pat goffi. coffi → goffi as the object.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I didn't get any post yesterday.

Ches i ddim post ddoe.

C → Ch marks the negative. 'ddim' blocks the object mutation, so post stays post.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Cawson nhw fargen yn y siop ddillad.

They got a bargain in the clothes shop.

bargen → fargen.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

We went to the beach. We had fine weather.

Aethon ni i'r traeth. Cawson ni dywydd braf.

tywydd → dywydd as the object.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Gest ti frechdan yn y swyddfa? Naddo.

Did you have a sandwich in the office? No.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Two sets of forms: The book gives both — learn the one your tutor uses, recognise the other. Ces i / Cest ti / Cafodd e,hi / Cawson ni / Cawsoch chi / Cawson nhw or Ges i / Gest ti / Gaeth e,hi / Gaethon ni / Gaethoch chi / Gaethon nhw.
  • Questions: C softens to G — Gest ti? Gawsoch chi? Gafodd e? Answer Do / Naddo, as for every short-form past.
  • Negative (Treiglad Llaes): C → Ch: Ches i ddim. T, C, P verbs do the same (Thalais i ddim, Phrynais i ddim); other letters soften (Ddarllenais i ddim). 'ddim' stops the object mutating: Ces i goffi but Ches i ddim coffi.
Uned 12 Mae car gyda fi (Having) ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I have a car.

Mae car gyda fi.

Literally 'there is a car with me'. Spoken: Mae car 'da fi.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Oes plant gyda chi? Nac oes.

Have you got children? No.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I don't have a dog.

Does dim ci gyda fi.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Mae dau o blant gyda hi.

She has two children.

plant → blant after 'o'.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Has Tomos got a car? -> Yes.

Oes car gyda Tomos? Oes.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Faint o blant sy gyda fe?

How many children does he have?

'sy' (not 'mae') after Faint.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I have one daughter.

Mae un ferch gyda fi.

merch is feminine, so it softens after 'un'. un mab doesn't.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Does dim amser gyda ni.

We haven't got time.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Possession: Welsh says 'there is X with me'. Mae car gyda fi / ti / fe / hi / ni / chi / nhw. With a name: Mae brawd gyda Bethan.
  • Question and negative: Oes car gyda ti? → Oes / Nac oes. Negative: Does dim car gyda fi.
  • Counting: number + o + plural (softened): dau o blant. Or number + singular, minding gender: un mab / un ferch, dau fab / dwy ferch, tri mab / tair merch, pedwar mab / pedair merch.
Uned 13 Roedd hi'n braf (Was - Roedd) ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

It was fine yesterday.

Roedd hi'n braf ddoe.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Oedd hi'n oer neithiwr? Nac oedd.

Was it cold last night? No.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

The food wasn't good.

Doedd y bwyd ddim yn dda.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Roedd pen tost gyda fi.

I had a headache.

A part of the body hurting uses gyda.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

She had a cold.

Roedd annwyd arni hi.

An illness 'on' a person uses ar.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Roedd clust dost gyda fe.

He had earache.

clust is feminine, so tost → dost.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

How was the weather yesterday?

Sut roedd y tywydd ddoe?

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Doedd e ddim yn hapus achos roedd e'n hwyr.

He wasn't happy because he was late.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Roedd / Oedd / Doedd: Roedd hi'n braf (It was fine). Question: Oedd hi'n braf? → Oedd / Nac oedd. Negative: Doedd hi ddim yn braf.
  • Gyda vs Ar: Pain in a body part takes gyda: Mae cefn tost gyda fi. An illness takes ar: Mae annwyd arna i. ar changes: arna i, arnat ti, arno fe, arni hi, arnon ni, arnoch chi, arnyn nhw.
  • Mutations: Adjectives soften after 'yn' (Roedd hi'n drist) and after a singular feminine noun (clust + tost = clust dost).
Uned 14 Ble ro'ch chi'n byw? (Used to - Ro'n i) ›
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I live in Tregaron.

Dw i'n byw yn Nhregaron.

Nasal mutation after 'yn' (in): T → Nh.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Ro'n i'n byw ym Mhenarth pan o'n i'n blentyn.

I lived in Penarth when I was a child.

P → Mh, and yn becomes ym.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

Where were you last night? (Informal)

Ble ro't ti neithiwr?

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Ro'n i'n gwrando ar y radio am saith o'r gloch.

I was listening to the radio at seven o'clock.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

I used to work in a factory.

Ro'n i'n (arfer) gweithio mewn ffatri.

'arfer' is optional. No mutation after mewn.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

O't ti'n hoffi cinio ysgol? Nac o'n.

Did you like school dinners? No.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

We used to work in Carmarthen.

Ro'n ni'n gweithio yng Nghaerfyrddin.

C → Ngh, and yn becomes yng.
CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Do'n i ddim yn gwybod.

I didn't know.

hoffi/lico, gwybod/nabod and eisiau always take this tense.
Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Treiglad Trwynol (Nasal Mutation) after 'yn' (in): T → yn Nh, D → yn N, C → yng Ngh, G → yng Ng, P → ym Mh, B → ym M. 'yn' itself changes too. No mutation after mewn (in a).
  • Ro'n i (was / used to): Ro'n i, Ro't ti, Roedd e/hi, Ro'n ni, Ro'ch chi, Ro'n nhw. Question: O'n i? O't ti? O'ch chi? Negative: Do'n i ddim. Answers: O'n / Nac o'n (and O't, Oedd, O'ch).
  • Which past? One-off event: Cerddais i i'r ysgol ddoe. Repeated or ongoing: Ro'n i'n cerdded i'r ysgol pan o'n i'n blentyn. Always Ro'n i with hoffi, gwybod, nabod, eisiau: Ro'n i eisiau (no 'n).

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Wyt ti wedi cael brecwast? Ydw.

Have you had breakfast? Yes.

wedi questions are answered like the present: Ydw / Nac ydw.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

dod → ddod after the short-form verb.
SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

'Dyn ni'n mynd ar wyliau ym mis Gorffennaf.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Oes copi gyda chi, os gwelwch chi'n dda?

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Perfect tense (have done): swap the linking 'yn' for wedi. Dw i'n darllen (I am reading) → Dw i wedi darllen (I have read). Negative: Dw i ddim wedi...
  • Three pasts: Darllenais i (I read), Ro'n i'n darllen (I was reading), Dw i wedi darllen (I have read).
  • Misoedd: Ionawr, Chwefror, Mawrth, Ebrill, Mai, Mehefin, Gorffennaf, Awst, Medi, Hydref, Tachwedd, Rhagfyr. 'In August' = ym mis Awst.

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Ydy'r bwyd yn ddrud? Nac ydy, ddim yn rhy ddrud.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Time uses 'hi': Mae hi'n bump o'r gloch. Numbers soften after yn, i and am: Mae hi'n ddau, chwarter i bedwar, am bump.
  • Old numbers for time: 20 = ugain, 25 = pum munud ar hugain. munud and awr sit in the middle: pedair awr ar hugain (24 hours).
  • Money: punt and ceiniog are feminine: punt, dwy bunt, tair punt, pedair punt, pum punt. tri and chwe cause the Treiglad Llaes: chwe phunt, tri phlentyn.

SAESNEG i GYMRAEG

CYMRAEG i SAESNEG

Pryd bydd e'n ôl?

When will he be back?

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • Future of bod: Bydd e/hi (he/she/it will be), Fydd e/hi? (will...?), Fydd e/hi ddim (won't be). Also Bydda i, Fydda i?, Fydda i ddim.
  • Answers: Fydd hi'n braf? → Bydd / Na fydd.
  • Same 'yn' rules as always: verbs don't mutate, nouns and adjectives do. Bydd hi'n gweithio / Bydd hi'n diwtor / Bydd hi'n brysur.

Y Gornel Ieithyddol (Linguistic Help)
  • The whole future: Bydda i, Byddi di, Bydd e/hi, Byddwn ni, Byddwch chi, Byddan nhw. Questions soften B → F: Fyddi di? Negative: Fydda i ddim.
  • Answers follow the verb: Fyddi di? → Bydda / Na fydda. Fyddwch chi? → Bydda (I) or Byddwn (we). Fyddan nhw? → Byddan / Na fyddan.
  • Os and ar gyfer: Os bydd hi'n braf, bydda i'n... (If it's fine, I'll...). ar gyfer = for: dillad ar gyfer y parti.
